# Self-Directed Learning Products & Teaching Methods: Engagement Survey

Research for an AI "cognitive sparring partner" teaching linear algebra to an adult autodidact with irregular, energy-driven sessions; success = still doing it at 3 months.

---

## 1. Brilliant

1. **Core engagement mechanic**: Gamification layered on problem-solving-first pedagogy — points, levels, streaks, leaderboards, timed challenges, and Rive-animated feedback (a dedicated "User Motivation" team owns this). [Trophy.so case study](https://trophy.so/blog/brilliant-gamification-case-study), [Rive blog](https://rive.app/blog/how-brilliant-org-motivates-learners-with-rive-animations)
2. **Unit of a session**: Short interactive "problem" within a course/path; users attempt, get immediate feedback and hints, accumulate points toward a daily goal.
3. **Re-entry after a gap**: UNVERIFIED — no primary source found describing a specific re-entry flow (vs. Duolingo's explicit mercy mechanics); marketing material is silent on this.
4. **Too-easy material**: Adaptive algorithms tune problem difficulty to individual performance ("personalized difficulty tuning"), but no public engineering detail on the placement/skip algorithm. [Trophy.so](https://trophy.so/blog/brilliant-gamification-case-study)
5. **Stuck learner**: Immediate feedback + hints embedded in problems; no escalation protocol documented.
6. **Failure modes / criticism**: User reviews describe the content as "rote," "shallow," good for intro/review but "too light-touch" for real depth — useful for revisiting known material, weak for learning genuinely new material. [Brighterly review roundup](https://brighterly.com/blog/is-brilliant-org-worth-it/) (secondary source, UNVERIFIED as representative).

## 2. Math Academy

1. **Core engagement mechanic**: No gamification layer of note — the pitch is raw efficiency (claimed 4x the pace of traditional courses) via a from-scratch adaptive engine: knowledge graph (thousands of topics, tens of thousands of edges) + spaced repetition + task-selection algorithm + adaptive diagnostic. [mathacademy.com/how-our-ai-works](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-our-ai-works), founder Justin Skycak's blog [justinmath.com](https://www.justinmath.com/blog/)
2. **Unit of a session**: Fine-grained "knowledge points" (~10x more granular than typical curricula) — each a worked example + practice set, with explicit "subgoal labels." Reviews are compressed via **macro-interleaving** (mixed topics per session) and **FIRe** (Fractional Implicit Repetition — advanced-topic practice implicitly reinforces prerequisites, cutting redundant review). [frankhecker.com part 5](https://frankhecker.com/2025/02/12/math-academy-part-5/), [FIRe paper](https://www.justinmath.com/individualized-spaced-repetition-in-hierarchical-knowledge-structures/)
3. **Re-entry after a gap**: Spaced-repetition engine auto-schedules reviews based on a per-student-per-topic "learning speed" multiplier (0.5x–2x); specific cold-start-after-long-gap protocol not separately documented (UNVERIFIED beyond general SRS behavior).
4. **Too-easy material**: "Layering" — advance to new topics the instant prerequisite mastery is shown; within-lesson difficulty adapts so quizzes average ~80% accuracy, with stronger students getting harder variants and needing fewer questions per task.
5. **Stuck learner**: System **halts the lesson** once further struggle is judged unproductive, redirects to unrelated topics, and on repeated failure injects **remedial review** of the specific prerequisite the knowledge graph flags as the bottleneck — not just "try again" or "make it easier" in place.
6. **Failure modes / criticism**: Pace and intensity are the recurring complaint — "we do too much in not enough time" (Pershan critique), no video hand-holding, trickier questions; some adult self-learners report being "extremely overwhelmed." Commercial incentive to accelerate (flat monthly fee) is noted as a possible bias toward recommending speed. [HN thread 1](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43135664), [HN thread 2](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41017830), [balanced review](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43656481)

## 3. 3Blue1Brown (and Summer of Math Exposition)

1. **Core engagement mechanic**: Not an engagement product — a single-creator video essay channel. Mechanic is pure content quality: visual intuition built through an imagined "path of discovery" rather than definition-first exposition; motivation from "story and intrinsic delight," not applied utility. [Dwarkesh interview](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/grant-sanderson), [Stanford Daily](https://stanforddaily.com/2020/01/24/3blue1brown-creator-grant-sanderson-15-talks-engaging-with-math-using-stories-and-visuals/)
2. **Unit of a session**: One video (~10–20 min), explicitly positioned as a "supplemental intuition builder," not a replacement for coursework/practice.
3. **Re-entry after a gap**: N/A — no session state, no user accounts; it's broadcast media, not a learning system.
4. **Too-easy material**: N/A — no adaptivity; viewer self-selects by topic/series.
5. **Stuck learner**: N/A — no interactivity or feedback loop; SoME (Summer of Math Exposition, a contest Sanderson runs for creators, not learners) explicitly values topic choice, a single strong visual insight, or an unexpected application over uniform polish — "you shouldn't be worried about a need for it to be excellent in all respects." [SoME1 results](https://www.3blue1brown.com/blog/some1-results/)
6. **Failure modes / criticism**: Well-documented "illusion of understanding" critique — viewers feel they understand after a slick 10-minute video but cannot do the actual math because there's no homework/testing of the feeling of understanding; videos build intuition but not skill, and are widely noted as needing to be paired with active problem-solving. [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=35714228), [Quantum Formalism critique](https://quantumformalism.substack.com/p/deep-maths-learning-beyond-the-sleek)

## 4. Execute Program

1. **Core engagement mechanic**: No gamification — trust-based design (Gary Bernhardt/Destroy All Software pedigree) addressing "uncertainty about the author's expertise, the tool, and whether you'll be able to apply what you learned." Mechanic is simply: write real code, immediately, in every lesson. [andymatuschak.org notes](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Execute_Program), [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42278400)
2. **Unit of a session**: Hundreds of small interactive code examples per course, slowly increasing complexity, mixing explanation with "write and run real code" checks; ~20 min/day for a couple weeks per course, then review sessions shrink from ~10 min/day toward zero as spacing widens.
3. **Re-entry after a gap**: Spaced-repetition scheduler resumes due reviews; a close reader (Andy Matuschak) notes a specific design choice — **missed answers don't penalize the student unless they "give up"** (distinguishes slow recall from true forgetting). [andymatuschak.org](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zX7RdiHGbHYnkFznUr9VZx7)
4. **Too-easy material**: UNVERIFIED — no documented skip/placement mechanism; courses appear linear, not adaptive to prior knowledge.
5. **Stuck learner**: Explanatory text + runnable examples precede each check; no documented escalation/hint ladder beyond "give up and see the answer" (which does count against the review item per above).
6. **Failure modes / criticism**: A long-time user (4 months) who considers Execute Program's teaching "incredibly well done" nonetheless calls its SRS implementation "crude relative to Anki" — no difficulty self-rating, arbitrary 4-correct-reviews cutoff regardless of confidence, and related items scheduled same-day so review feels "artificially easy." Main complaint about content: "they're not longer and there aren't more of them" (content-supply bottleneck, not methodology). [mike.place](https://mike.place/2020/executeprogram/)

## 5. Khan Academy (including Khanmigo)

1. **Core engagement mechanic (base platform)**: Mastery learning (Bloom-inspired) — must demonstrate proficiency before advancing; "energy points" for effort (not mastery), badges (Meteorite → Moon → Earth → Sun → Black Hole), streaks. [support.khanacademy.org](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/202487710-What-are-energy-points-badges-and-avatars)
2. **Unit of a session**: Video + practice exercise set per skill, with per-unit mastery levels (Apprentice 0–24% → Explorer → Pioneer → Adventurer → Sage 100%).
3. **Re-entry after a gap**: UNVERIFIED in primary sources surveyed — mastery system implies review/decay mechanics exist but no engineering blog post found detailing the re-entry algorithm specifically.
4. **Too-easy material**: Mastery-based skip-ahead — placement and unit tests let students test out of material they already know (standard mastery-learning design); no adaptive-within-problem detail found beyond this.
5. **Stuck learner (base platform)**: "Get a Hint" reveals solution steps incrementally on request, but using a hint marks the item as not-mastered even if completed — discourages hint-as-shortcut. [support.khanacademy.org](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/community/posts/360056019091-Warning-Abusing-Hints-Cheating-Strategy)
6. **Khanmigo design + failure modes**: Khanmigo is explicitly Socratic by prompt design — "will ask questions to try to lead the student through what they know... give support and potentially a step to take, but not the answer itself." [Khan Academy engineering blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-we-built-ai-tutoring-tools/) **Field result (University of Toronto 2-year RCT, 18 Tennessee middle schools, 2024–2026)**: 96% tried it once, but median student messaged it on only ~1/3 of practice days and in only 17% of sessions with a mistake; **when Khanmigo refused direct answers and asked questions instead, students "largely stopped using it"** — they wanted an answer machine, not a tutor; AI added no measurable gain over Khan Academy's non-AI practice alone. Khan Academy's response was to redesign so "AI could not just sit next to the content, it had to be woven into it" (Sal Khan). [Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2026/08/25/ai-tutoring-students-khanmigo-khan-academy-engagement-study/), [Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-khanmigo-math-ai-tutor/), [EdWorkingPaper](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1551.pdf). Separately, Common Sense Media (Aug 2025) found Khanmigo "sometimes responded in ways that amplified and reinforced unfair gender biases and stereotypes" (UNVERIFIED primary report, cited secondhand). A 2024 Wharton study (different unrestricted-AI-tutor context, not Khanmigo specifically) found students scored 48% better *with* AI assistance during practice but 17% *worse* on unassisted exams — an "AI as crutch" effect relevant to any assistant that over-helps.

## 6. Duolingo

1. **Core engagement mechanic**: The canonical streak/habit loop — daily streak counter, XP, leagues, loss aversion over the "endowed" streak asset, and heavily personalized push notifications via a bandit algorithm balancing message novelty against predicted open-likelihood. [Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/insights/consumer-insights/streak-creep-the-perils-of-too-much-gamification), [ngrow.ai](https://www.ngrow.ai/blog/the-impact-of-push-notifications-on-customer-retention-insights-from-duolingo)
2. **Unit of a session**: One short lesson (minutes), gated to a daily-goal XP target.
3. **Re-entry after a gap**: Explicit multi-layer "mercy infrastructure" — (a) **Streak Freeze** (consumable item, auto-applied if held, preserves streak across a single missed day), (b) **Streak Repair** (pay gems after the fact to restore a just-broken streak), (c) **Amnesty/"Earn Back" events** for streaks broken 30+ days ago, restorable by completing 3 lessons in a row. Streak breaks are designed as a *recoverable state*, not a terminal one. [Duolingo Fandom wiki](https://duolingo.fandom.com/wiki/Shop/Streak_freeze), [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/duolingo-revive-broken-streak-event-3673004/)
4. **Too-easy material**: Placement test at signup to skip known units; otherwise mostly fixed-path curriculum, not deeply adaptive within a lesson (UNVERIFIED in depth here — out of this survey's focus since Duolingo is cited for habit design, not pedagogy).
5. **Stuck learner**: Immediate right/wrong feedback, limited hearts (mistake budget) in some versions, "explanation" tips; not a Socratic/tutoring system.
6. **Failure modes / criticism**: Widely documented **guilt-based notification design** — "You made Duo sad 😢" outperforms neutral copy by 5–8% in open rate; critics call this "weaponizing guilt" once the streak alone stops compelling use. **Streak-over-substance effect**: a 2023 *Journal of Consumer Research* finding that users treat extending the streak as more important than the underlying activity, and that breaking a streak predicts quitting the platform entirely (not gradual decay) — i.e., the mechanic that drives retention also creates a cliff-edge failure mode. Users self-report "Duolingo burnout" — maintaining 500+ day streaks "out of habit" while learning little, and preferring the gamified app over harder-but-more-effective practice (e.g., real conversation). On pedagogy: independent (non-Duolingo-commissioned) research is thin; studies found weak gains in speaking skill specifically, and speech-recognition inaccuracy actively impeding pronunciation feedback. [Medium: "guilt algorithm"](https://medium.com/@milessightings/i-reverse-engineered-duolingos-guilt-algorithm-6ddf598d2a72) (couldn't fetch directly, 403 — claims via secondary search summary, treat specifics as UNVERIFIED), [Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/insights/consumer-insights/streak-creep-the-perils-of-too-much-gamification), [Foreign Language Annals study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/flan.12600)

## 7. Anki and spaced-repetition systems (FSRS)

1. **Core engagement mechanic**: None by design — pure utility tool; "engagement" is whatever external habit the user supplies. The product-level mechanic is algorithmic trust: minimize wasted reviews while hitting a target retention rate.
2. **Unit of a session**: The "card" (atomic review item); a session is just "clear the due queue," arbitrary length.
3. **Re-entry after a gap**: FSRS models memory per-card via **stability** (resistance to forgetting) and **difficulty**, and a **post-lapse stability formula** recomputes the schedule after a lapse/gap using prior history rather than resetting — includes "difficulty mean reversion" specifically to avoid cards getting permanently stuck in a hard, over-reviewed state ("ease hell" in SM-2). [LessWrong: History of FSRS](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/G7fpGCi8r7nCKXsQk/the-history-of-fsrs-for-anki)
4. **Too-easy material**: User self-authors/selects cards; no content-level adaptivity, only review-timing adaptivity (cards you know well get long intervals automatically).
5. **Stuck learner**: Failing a card ("Again") shortens its interval and increases review frequency; no pedagogical scaffolding — SRS schedules review, it doesn't teach.
6. **Failure modes / criticism**: SM-2 (Anki's old default) criticized for a single global ease factor that spirals downward irreversibly ("ease hell"), ignoring individual memory variation, and treating all "Good" ratings identically regardless of how close the item was to being forgotten. FSRS (default since Nov 2023, FSRS-6 trained on ~700M community reviews as of late 2025) fixes most of this and cuts review time 20–40% at equal retention, but its own calibration metric proved "hackable" (optimizing the visible calibration graph without real improvement, forcing reliance on log-loss instead), sibling cards cluster together unless specifically dispersed, and some users show non-monotonic forgetting curves the model doesn't fit well, especially with low review-count histories. [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/G7fpGCi8r7nCKXsQk/the-history-of-fsrs-for-anki), [Expertium benchmark blog](https://expertium.github.io/Benchmark.html)

## 8. Art of Problem Solving (Alcumus and classes)

1. **Core engagement mechanic**: Mastery-via-challenge for an already-motivated, advanced-math-inclined population — no streaks/points-as-primary-driver; the draw is genuinely hard, well-explained problems and a community of peers (forums, competitions). [AoPS blog](https://artofproblemsolving.com/blog/articles/what-is-alcumus-why-we-called-it-that)
2. **Unit of a session (Alcumus)**: One problem at a time within a chosen "focus topic"; correct answers get a full worked explanation (often multiple solution paths), not just "Correct!"
3. **Re-entry after a gap**: UNVERIFIED — no primary source found on spaced re-review scheduling; Alcumus appears to track topic-mastery state persistently but re-entry-specific behavior is undocumented in what was retrieved.
4. **Too-easy material**: Fully adaptive within a topic — gets harder as the student succeeds, easier (more foundational) if struggling; topic-based rather than whole-curriculum pacing, so a strong student burns through a focus topic fast then must pick a new one (no fully automatic curriculum-wide acceleration documented). [AoPS wiki: Focus Topic](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/Focus_Topic)
5. **Stuck learner**: Lets students submit a wrong answer, then **shows that wrong answer alongside a second attempt** (so they don't blindly repeat the same mistake) before unlocking the full solution; solutions link back to AoPS textbook sections/videos for the needed concept.
6. **Failure modes / criticism (classes, not Alcumus)**: Pace criticized as very fast — "blazingly fast," a full Algebra 1+ course compressed into 16 weeks, advanced topics (negative/fractional exponents) introduced with little preceding review. More pointed: a community critic argued "recommending AoPS to struggling students is simply irresponsible," noting staff backgrounds skew toward competition-math pedigree, not remediation of struggling learners — AoPS is "a paradise for a particular group of students," not a general-purpose curriculum. [Well-Trained Mind forum](https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/206721-art-of-problem-solving-too-hard/), [Gifted Issues forum](https://giftedissues.davidsongifted.org/bb/ubbthreads.php/ubb/printthread/Board/5/main/7614/type/thread.html)

## 9. Project Euler and Advent of Code

1. **Core engagement mechanic**: Pure intrinsic/community-status motivation — no streaks, no spaced repetition, no adaptive difficulty. Project Euler: solved-problem count + a public rank ladder (Tetrahedron → Cube → Octahedron → Dodecahedron → Icosahedron → Sphere, top 100). Advent of Code: one themed puzzle/day in December + (through 2024) a global speed leaderboard. [PE forum: Member Ranks](https://projecteuler.chat/viewtopic.php?t=8051), [AoC Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advent_of_Code)
2. **Unit of a session**: One self-contained problem, solved at the user's own pace in their own language/tools; no imposed time box (except AoC's global-leaderboard speed race, now discontinued).
3. **Re-entry after a gap**: None needed by design — fully asynchronous, no streak, no state decay; a user can vanish for years and resume exactly where their solved-list left off.
4. **Too-easy material**: Self-selected — Project Euler problems are numbered roughly by submission order with informal/crowd-sourced difficulty ratings so users jump around; no adaptive placement, pure self-selection.
5. **Stuck learner**: Forums per-problem (locked until *you* solve it, to prevent spoilers) are the only support; otherwise the learner is simply stuck until they solve it or quit the problem. This is close to the inverse of a tutoring system.
6. **Failure modes / criticism**: Project Euler's rank ladder was criticized because higher ranks can take a year-plus of solving to reach, "removing much of the feedback/reward" for an individual hard problem — too slow a reward loop; PE later rebalanced rank-population sizes (adding a "Spheres" tier, widening lower tiers) to restore felt progress. Advent of Code's **global leaderboard became a genuine failure mode**: by its 10th year (2025) creator Eric Wastl discontinued it entirely, citing (a) competitive abuse up to and including DDoS attacks over leaderboard standing, and (b) LLM-era "cheesing" — feeding the puzzle text straight to an AI and submitting its output, which hollowed out the speed-race's meaning; Wastl simultaneously cut the event from 25 days to 12, citing creator burnout and the well-known community pattern of "most people fall off sharply after day 7–10" under the daily-for-a-month cadence. [BigGo](https://biggo.com/news/202510271323_Advent-of-Code-2025-Changes), [Hachyderm/Eric Wastl](https://hachyderm.io/@ericwastl/115415473413415697)

## 10. Moore method and inquiry-based learning

1. **Core engagement mechanic**: Radical ownership — students prove theorems themselves from a bare sequence of definitions/problems with no textbook and minimal lecture, then present proofs to peers who critique them; engagement comes from authorship and public accountability to the group, not external reward. [legacyrlmoore.org](http://legacyrlmoore.org/reference/mahavier1.html)
2. **Unit of a session**: One class meeting centered on student presentations of problems assigned since the last meeting; the "note set" (a crafted sequence of definitions and problems) is the curriculum unit, not a lesson or video.
3. **Re-entry after a gap**: N/A/UNVERIFIED — designed for a fixed-cohort, fixed-schedule semester course, not for irregular self-paced re-entry; no literature found addressing gap re-entry specifically.
4. **Too-easy material**: Handled entirely by instructor judgment — the instructor "constantly monitor[s] the progress of individual students and adjust[s] the notes" to assign harder problems to students moving faster; no automated adaptivity, it's hand-curated per student by a human.
5. **Stuck learner**: Core philosophy (R. L. Moore's original) is that a student learns best **alone, without help** — hints withheld by design to force independent struggle; Mahavier's "modified" version is explicit that pure Moore is too harsh and adds that a student "sometimes needs a bit of help, encouragement, or reinforcement," delivered as hints written on returned papers or suggested individualized problems once the instructor knows the student well enough to calibrate. The intervention threshold is a judgment call, not a rule. [legacyrlmoore.org](http://legacyrlmoore.org/reference/mahavier1.html), [ERIC: Modified Moore Method](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED502664.pdf)
6. **Failure modes / criticism**: Documented student "antipathy" toward the method, concentrated especially among math-education (vs. pure math) majors who find the unsupported-struggle model punishing rather than motivating; "mixed reception by students and faculty" generally. No large-scale outcome studies found (UNVERIFIED at scale — evidence here is case-study/survey level, not RCTs).

## 11. Socratic/tutoring protocols: Bloom 2-sigma lineage and 2023–2026 AI tutor studies

1. **Core engagement mechanic (the claim)**: One-on-one tutoring + mastery learning (require ~90%+ mastery before advancing) was reported by Bloom (1984, *Educational Researcher*) to produce ~2 standard deviations of improvement over classroom instruction. [Wikipedia: Bloom's 2 sigma problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloom's_2_sigma_problem)
2. **Unit of a session**: In the original studies, a full tutoring/mastery-learning curriculum replacing a classroom unit, not a single short interaction — relevant because headline AI-tutor results (below) are typically measured over a single lesson or two, not a semester.
3. **Re-entry after a gap**: N/A in the classic literature — not a design studied in the tutoring-effect literature.
4. **Too-easy material / adaptivity**: Mastery learning's core adaptivity is pacing-to-criterion (don't advance until mastered); it says nothing about accelerating past material already known.
5. **Stuck learner**: The replication literature's actual recommended ingredients (vs. mastery learning per se) are: frequent granular feedback on errors, guided reasoning via questioning rather than direct correction, high repetition/exposure, and the "testing effect" (frequent low-stakes assessment itself drives learning). [Nintil systematic review](https://nintil.com/bloom-sigma/)
6. **Failure modes / criticism — the 2-sigma number does not replicate**: Nintil's review of the replication literature finds real effect sizes far below Bloom's d≈2.0 — general tutoring ≈0.79, software tutoring ≈0.79 (comparable to average human tutors, per VanLehn), mastery learning ≈0.4–0.52 on researcher-made tests but **near zero on independent standardized tests** (Slavin's best-evidence synthesis: "essentially nil"); effect sizes shrink as study rigor/sample size increases (d≈0.38 for n<100 vs. d≈0.11 for n>2000, suggesting publication bias); a 2020 Khan Academy RCT found "no meaningful effects"; and there's weak transfer — mastery of a narrow skill doesn't generalize broadly. The DARPA Digital Tutor is the one outlier success (d≈1.97–3.18, Navy techs trained in 16 weeks vs. 35), attributed to deep domain-expert authored content plus high-fidelity practice, not tutoring-format alone. **Harvard physics AI-tutor RCT (Kestin, Miller et al., *Scientific Reports*, June 2025)**: crossover design, n=194, intro physics; a custom GPT-4-based tutor with research-authored Socratic scaffolding ("behave like a seasoned instructor," never just give the answer) produced learning gains **more than double** the in-class active-learning condition, in less time (49 vs. 60 min), with self-reported higher engagement/motivation — but this is a single two-week, two-topic study at one elite institution, publication caveats explicit that AI tutors are meant to supplement, not replace, in-person instruction. [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2024/09/professor-tailored-ai-tutor-to-physics-course-engagement-doubled/) **Contrast — Khanmigo field RCT finds the opposite engagement pattern**: when a real, free-choice K-12 population (not a mandatory university study) got a Socratic AI tutor that withheld answers, most **stopped using it** rather than engaging more (see Khan Academy section above) — the single biggest tension in this literature: controlled studies with structured/mandatory use show large AI-Socratic-tutor gains, but voluntary real-world engagement with the same design pattern is the opposite of guaranteed. Broader 2024–2026 meta-analyses of GenAI tutoring report much more modest, highly heterogeneous pooled effects (g≈0.45–0.67, I²≈95%) and specifically find **teacher/structure co-presence roughly doubles effect size** (g=1.43 with support vs. g=0.08 without) — i.e., an unsupervised AI tutor in the wild is a substantially weaker intervention than the same model embedded in a structured, accountable setting. [Nature meta-analysis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-026-07019-z), [ScienceDirect K-12 meta-analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2451958826000473)

## Patterns

**Recurring design patterns in the successful/credible systems:**

1. **Granular units + mastery-gating before advancement** (Khan Academy, Math Academy, Alcumus, FSRS/Anki at the card level) — nobody moves forward on an unmastered prerequisite.
2. **Difficulty adapts to performance in both directions, within a narrow topic window**, not a single global level (Math Academy's 80%-accuracy targeting, Alcumus's topic-local adaptivity, Brilliant's per-problem tuning).
3. **Struggle is redirected, not just repeated** — halt-and-switch-topic-then-remediate (Math Academy) beats "show an easier version of the same problem" or infinite retry.
4. **Recoverable, non-punitive re-entry state** — Duolingo's freeze/repair/amnesty layers and FSRS's post-lapse-stability recomputation both treat a gap as a parameter update, not a reset to zero or a shame event (contrast: Duolingo's *messaging* around gaps is punitive even though its *mechanics* are forgiving).
5. **Immediate, explanation-rich feedback beats correctness-only feedback** — Alcumus shows the wrong answer back before a retry and gives multi-path explanations even on success; Execute Program pairs every check with runnable code; this recurs as the single most load-bearing ingredient across the tutoring-replication literature (Nintil).
6. **Self-selected, asynchronous, state-independent progress systems have zero re-entry problem** because they never built a re-entry *cost* in the first place (Project Euler, Advent of Code via private leaderboards, Anki content authorship) — the "gap" problem is partly an artifact of streak/calendar-based design, not an inherent feature of learning systems.
7. **Peer/community accountability substitutes for engineered gamification** in intrinsically-motivated populations (Moore method's presentation-to-peers, Project Euler/AoC forums, SoME's Discord) — these systems have essentially no retention engineering and still sustain multi-year engagement in their target population.
8. **Hint ladders that cost something** (Khan Academy hints void mastery credit; Alcumus shows your own wrong answer before offering the solution) outperform free/instant answer reveals at preserving the productive-struggle window.

**Recurring anti-patterns in the documented failures:**

1. **Withholding answers from a free-choice population kills engagement even when it helps learning** — Khanmigo's Socratic refusal to answer directly caused most students to simply disengage, the sharpest documented mismatch between "pedagogically correct" and "behaviorally sustainable" (Khanmigo field RCT), and the exact opposite of what the mandatory-use Harvard RCT showed — mandatory vs. voluntary context flips the result.
2. **Engagement metrics that diverge from the underlying activity become the goal and then collapse catastrophically** — Duolingo streaks: users optimize the streak itself, report burnout, and a broken streak predicts total platform abandonment rather than graceful decay (streak-over-substance effect, Journal of Consumer Research).
3. **Headline effect sizes from small/favorable studies evaporate under rigor and independent measurement** — Bloom's 2-sigma (d≈2.0 claimed, d≈0.4–0.8 replicated, ~0 on independent standardized tests) and Khan Academy's own 2020 RCT ("no meaningful effects") both show the persistent large-claim/small-replication gap in this field; treat any single-study AI-tutor result (including the Harvard physics RCT) with the same discount until replicated.
4. **Competitive/leaderboard mechanics invite gaming and abuse that eventually force the mechanic's removal** — Advent of Code's global leaderboard was killed after a decade due to DDoS-level leaderboard abuse and LLM-assisted cheating, and Project Euler's rank ladder had to be rebalanced because reward latency (a year-plus between rank-ups) gutted its own motivational purpose.
5. **Pace/intensity tuned for the system's most able or most committed users alienates everyone else** — Math Academy's and AoPS's own communities flag the identical complaint (too fast, too little hand-holding, actively unsuitable for struggling or less-intense learners) despite being among the most pedagogically rigorous systems surveyed, warning that "efficient" and "sustainable for an irregular adult learner" are not the same design target.
