# Two views, one thing

## Commit

Take the reach question from lesson 3: a·(2, 1) + b·(1, 1) = (x, y).

1. Write it as equations in a and b. How many equations, how many unknowns, and what does each equation say in the picture?
2. When you solved 2a + b = 0 and a + b = 5 in school, what were you secretly doing with arrows?

Give a confidence from 1 to 4.

## Truth

1. Two equations, 2a + b = x and a + b = y, in two unknowns a and b. Each equation is one coordinate: the horizontal steps of the walk must total x, and the vertical steps must total y. The unknowns are how far to walk along each arrow.
2. You were finding the combination of the arrows (2, 1) and (1, 1) that lands on (0, 5). The columns of coefficients are the arrows; the right-hand side is the destination; elimination is a procedure for walking the skewed grid.

Lesson 4 in this language: the system 2a + 4b = 0, a + 2b = 5 has no solution because its column arrows lie on one line that misses (0, 5).

## Repair

The equations 2a + b = x and a + b = y are the walk read one coordinate at a time: the first totals the sideways steps, the second the upward steps. Solving 2a + b = 0 and a + b = 5 in school was choosing how far to walk along (2, 1) and (1, 1) to land on (0, 5), which is lesson 3's answer, a = −5 and b = 10.

## Exercises

1. Turn the system 3a − b = 4, a + 2b = 1 into an arrow question: name the arrows and the destination. Solve it both ways and check the answers agree.
2. Name one thing outside geometry that you can add and scale, and one thing that you cannot (postal codes, for example), and say what goes wrong for the second.
3. In your own words: what did this chapter break for you? If nothing broke, say what you would have bet on before lesson 3.

## Twist

Without solving, use arrows to decide whether 2a + b = 0 and 4a + 2b = 5 has a solution.
