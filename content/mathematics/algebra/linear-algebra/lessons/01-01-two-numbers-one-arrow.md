# Two numbers, one arrow

## Commit

Two parts.

1. An arrow starts at the origin and ends at the point (3, 1). Write it as a column of two numbers.
2. A second arrow starts at (1, 2) and ends at (4, 3). Is it the same vector as the first, or a different one?

Give both answers and a confidence from 1 (guessing) to 4 (certain).

## Truth

1. The column is (3, 1).
2. The same vector. From (1, 2) to (4, 3) is 4 − 1 = 3 steps right and 3 − 2 = 1 step up, so its column is also (3, 1).

Draw both arrows. They are parallel, equally long, and point the same way. Sliding an arrow without turning or stretching it does not change the vector, because the vector is the trip, not where the trip started. Drawing every arrow from the origin is a convention, and a useful one: then the coordinates of the tip equal the entries of the column.

## Repair

The second arrow starts somewhere else but makes the same trip. Subtracting start from end, (4, 3) − (1, 2), strips away the starting point and leaves (3, 1), the first arrow's column. The column never recorded where an arrow begins, so moving the start cannot change it.

## Exercises

1. An arrow runs from (−2, 5) to (1, −1). Write its column.
2. Describe the arrow for the column (0, −4) in words, without using any numbers.
3. In your own words: why is "go 3 km east and 1 km north" a vector, while "the town hall" is not?

## Twist

An arrow from P to Q has column (3, 1), and you are told Q is the origin. Where is P? Then write the column of the arrow from Q back to P and name, in one word, how it relates to the first arrow.
